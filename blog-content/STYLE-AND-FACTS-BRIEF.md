## 3. POST STRUCTURE

**Hook (2–4 sentences).** Open with the reader's actual experience, a common misconception, or a concrete question. Never open with "In today's world" or "Have you ever wondered."

**Body sections (H2s).** The chapter you're given has exactly four lessons. Those four lessons are your content spine — but **do not use the lesson titles as headings.** Translate each into a reader-facing H2 that answers a question a member would ask. Use H3s inside where a section needs breaking up.

**One honest-limits moment.** Somewhere in every post, say what is not known, what is oversold, or where the evidence is thinner than the marketing. This is Revive's differentiator. If the chapter covers something the industry overclaims, name it plainly.

**## The Revive Tip** — required, exactly one per post, near the end. 70–130 words. It must:
- give a specific action the reader can take *at Revive*, tied to this post's topic
- name a real service from the list above
- contain exactly one markdown link to the most contextually appropriate URL from the table above — vary these across posts, do not send everyone to /pricing
- sound like a coach talking, not an ad

**## What to read next** — one or two sentences pointing to the adjacent chapter's topic. Write it as a plain-language teaser, not a course reference.

## 4. WRITING RULES

- **Translate, don't dumb down.** Keep the real mechanism and the real terminology, then immediately explain it in plain words. "Your sarcoplasmic reticulum — the calcium storage tank inside a muscle fiber —" is right. Dropping the term entirely is wrong; leaving it unexplained is also wrong.
- **No medical claims.** Never write that anything cures, treats, heals, detoxes, or prevents disease. Use "may help," "is associated with," "the research suggests." When a topic edges into medical territory, say plainly that it's a conversation for a doctor.
- **No fake citations.** You may name real, well-established science (the Finnish sauna cohort studies, the ACSM position stands, DSHEA, the two-process model of sleep) but never invent a study, a statistic, a percentage, or a researcher. If you don't know a number, don't use one.
- **Second person.** "You," not "one" or "the client."
- **Short paragraphs.** 2–4 sentences. This is read on phones.
- **No emoji. No exclamation points.** No "unlock," "supercharge," "game-changer," "journey," "in today's fast-paced world," or "let's dive in."
- **Fort Wayne where it fits naturally** — winter cold, summer humidity, local running routes, the drive home from work. Don't force it into every post.
- **Every post stands alone.** A reader arriving from Google has not read the previous 40 posts.
- **Unique slugs and titles.** Never reuse one across the whole set.

## 6. THE HARDEST PART

Several of these courses are dry on paper — chemistry, anatomy, medical terminology. Your job is to find the real member question hiding inside each chapter and lead with it. A chapter on buffer systems and pH becomes "Why your muscles burn, and what that burn actually is." A chapter on connective tissue becomes "Why tendons take so much longer to heal than muscle." A chapter on medical prefixes becomes "How to actually understand what your doctor just told you."

If you cannot find the member question, you have not thought hard enough about the chapter. Every one of them has one.
