Skip breakfast and somewhere around hour four you notice it. Not weakness exactly — more like being slightly further back from the front of your own attention.

You are not running out of fuel. You are switching which fuel you prefer, and that changeover is a gradual slide governed by two hormones pulling in opposite directions.

## ATP is cash flow, not savings

Chemists predict whether a reaction will proceed on its own using **Gibbs free energy**, written as delta G. Negative means the reaction releases usable energy and runs without being pushed. Positive means something else has to pay for it.

The phrase "high-energy phosphate bond" has confused generations of students, because energy is not stored inside the bond like a spring. ATP releases energy when it is split because the products are more stable and because your cells deliberately hold ATP high and ADP low, keeping the reaction far from equilibrium. That is also why the textbook standard-state number understates it — no cell has ever operated under standard-state conditions.

So ATP is not a fuel reserve. You carry seconds of it. The reserves are glycogen and fat, and everything below is about moving between them.

## Why fat carries more energy per gram

Underneath all of metabolism sits **redox** chemistry. Oxidation means losing electrons; reduction means gaining them. Fuel molecules are electron-rich, and oxygen is electron-hungry, and the energy you live on comes from letting electrons fall from one toward the other through a series of controlled steps.

How much energy a fuel yields depends on how reduced it already is. A fatty acid is a long chain of carbons wearing hydrogens — packed with electrons still to give up. A carbohydrate carbon is already partly oxidized, carrying oxygen atoms of its own.

That single structural fact is the chemistry behind roughly nine calories per gram for fat against four for carbohydrate. It is not that fat is a different kind of energy. There are simply more electrons left to harvest.

## Beta-oxidation, and what ketones actually are

To burn a fatty acid, your cell activates it, ferries it into the mitochondrion, and then runs **beta-oxidation**: a repeating four-step cut that removes two carbons at a time from the end of the chain. Each pass releases one acetyl-CoA plus loaded electron carriers headed for the mitochondrial membrane.

When carbohydrate is scarce, acetyl-CoA starts arriving faster than the citric acid cycle can accept it, partly because the cycle's own intermediates are being pulled away to manufacture glucose. The liver condenses the surplus into **ketone bodies**, which travel in the blood and are used as fuel by your brain and muscle. Amino acid carbon skeletons feed into the same machinery at various points, which is the real reason prolonged underfueling costs muscle — your body will take protein apart for its carbon.

The honest limit here is large. Ketosis is a metabolic state, not a fat-loss mechanism. You can be in ketosis and gaining fat if you eat more than you spend. Exogenous ketone drinks do raise blood ketone levels, but evidence that this improves performance or body composition in ordinary training is thin and inconsistent, and it is sold far ahead of what it has shown. Anyone considering a ketogenic diet while taking diabetes medication, or with kidney or liver conditions, should be planning it with a physician rather than an influencer.

## The switch is a dial

The fed and fasted states are managed by **reciprocal regulation**, and it is one of the tidier pieces of design in the body.

After a meal, insulin says store: build glycogen, build fat, build protein, and shut down the pathways that break those things apart. In the fasted state, glucagon and adrenaline say release: break glycogen down, manufacture glucose from other carbon, mobilize fatty acids from fat tissue.

The elegance is that the same phosphorylation event usually switches one pathway on and its opposite off. Otherwise you would build and dismantle glycogen at the same time and burn the difference as heat.

Which means the changeover is a dial, not a lever that snaps at a specific hour. You are always burning some fat and some carbohydrate; what shifts is the ratio. Intermittent fasting works for the people it works for mostly because compressing the eating window makes them eat less. "Fat burning begins at hour sixteen" is a marketing sentence, not a metabolic threshold.

## The Revive Tip

The practical version: if you train early and unfed, do not make that the day you attempt something heavy and new. Fasted training is fine for easy conditioning or a moderate strength session. It is a poor setting for a maximum attempt or your first Hyrox class, because low glycogen availability shows up first in the highest-output efforts — the last third, where the class is decided. On those days, eat something with carbohydrate an hour beforehand and see whether the finish changes.
