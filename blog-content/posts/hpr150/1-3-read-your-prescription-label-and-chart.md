The label on the bottle says "1 tab PO BID PRN." The after-visit summary mentions hyperglycemia and a referral to nephrology. Nobody in the room explained either one, because to them those are ordinary words.

They are ordinary words. They are also learnable in about ten minutes, and knowing them means you walk into your next appointment with real questions instead of a vague sense of dread.

## Gut and hormone words

*Gastr-* stomach, *enter-* intestine, *hepat-* liver, *chole-* bile, *col-* colon, *pancreat-* pancreas.

**Gastritis** is stomach inflammation. **Gastroesophageal reflux** is stomach contents moving back up the esophagus. **Hepatic** simply means "of the liver," which is why liver enzymes get called hepatic panels. **Cholelithiasis** breaks into bile plus stone plus condition — gallstones.

The endocrine side runs on gland names plus what is too high or too low. *Thyro-* thyroid, *adren-* adrenal, *glyc-* sugar. **Hypothyroidism** is an underactive thyroid, **hyperthyroidism** an overactive one. **Hyperglycemia** is high blood sugar; **hypoglycemia** is low. **Endocrine** itself means secreting inward — glands that release hormones into the bloodstream rather than through a duct.

## Kidney, bladder, and reproductive words

Both Latin and Greek survived here: *ren-* and *nephr-* both mean kidney. *Ur-* refers to urine, *cyst-* to the bladder, *lith-* to a stone.

**Nephrolithiasis** is kidney stones. **Cystitis** is bladder inflammation. The suffix *-uria* names something found in urine, so **hematuria** is blood in the urine and **proteinuria** is protein in it. **Renal function** is how well the kidneys filter, usually estimated from a blood test.

On the reproductive side, *hyster-* uterus, *oophor-* ovary, *orchi-* testis, *mamm-* and *mast-* breast. Attach *-ectomy* and you get the removal procedures: hysterectomy, oophorectomy, mastectomy.

## Skin, blood, and immune words

*Derm-* and *cutane-* skin, *erythem-* redness, *hem-* blood, *leuk-* white, *thromb-* clot, *immun-* immune, *lymph-* lymphatic.

**Dermatitis** is inflamed skin. **Subcutaneous** means beneath the skin. In bloodwork, **anemia** is a deficiency of red blood cells or hemoglobin, **leukocytosis** is an elevated white cell count, and the suffix *-penia* means too few of something — **thrombocytopenia** is a low platelet count. **Autoimmune** conditions are ones where the immune system targets the body's own tissue.

## The shorthand on the label

**Routes** — how the drug gets in. *PO* by mouth, *SL* under the tongue, *IM* into muscle, *IV* into a vein, *SubQ* under the skin, plus topical and transdermal for creams and patches.

**Timing** — *BID* twice a day, *TID* three times, *QID* four times, *QHS* at bedtime, *PRN* as needed. That last one matters: PRN means the dose is available to you, not required of you.

**Amounts** — *mg* is a milligram, *mcg* is a microgram, and a microgram is one-thousandth of a milligram. Mixing those up is one of the classic medication errors, which is why the abbreviation "µg" is discouraged in favor of spelling out mcg.

**Drug names carry classes.** Generic names are built with shared endings so clinicians can recognize the family on sight: *-statin* for cholesterol-lowering statins, *-pril* for ACE inhibitors, *-sartan* for ARBs, *-olol* for beta blockers, *-prazole* for proton pump inhibitors, *-cillin* for penicillins. Two people on atorvastatin and rosuvastatin are on the same class of drug.

Brand names, by contrast, decode to nothing. They are built by marketing departments to sound reassuring and be easy to say.

## The honest limit on all of this

Recognizing a drug class is not the same as understanding your prescription. Dose, timing, interactions, and the reason it was chosen over an alternative are the whole ballgame, and none of that is in the name. Never adjust, skip, or stop a medication because of something you decoded on a label or read online. That decision belongs to the person who prescribed it or to your pharmacist, who is genuinely one of the most underused free resources in healthcare.

Same goes for supplements. "Natural" is a marketing category, not a safety category, and supplements can interact with prescriptions. Bring the whole list — prescriptions, over-the-counter, supplements — to one person who can look at all of it at once.
